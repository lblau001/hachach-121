v21 — the inverted round

Three options, 360×640, RTL, on the prototype's own ground; components from explorations/v16/prototype/hachach.css. Hebrew only inside the frames, annotations in English, striped yellow is unwritten copy. Nothing here is implemented. All three are OPEN.

The flaw in the previous draw — and yes, I agree it was the flaw

  • B-1, B-2 and B-3 printed the MK's name under each blurred face (בני גנץ, יאיר לפיד, איתמר בן-גביר), and B-1 step 3 added the parties. The blur then concealed nothing the player did not already have. They read the names and pick. The un-blur was decorative and the mechanic had no tension left in it. That is not a detail — it removes the reason for the round to exist.
  • The second problem was the question itself. What was drawn asked "which of these three voted בעד?" — which is the round the game already has, with a blur on it. Tamar's spec is the inverse: one blurred MK whose vote is stated, and the player guesses who it is.

Why hers is the stronger mechanic

  • It is genuinely a different game: the standard round asks how did this person vote, this asks who is this person. Recognition, which the research already identified as civic learning in its own right.
  • It makes the un-blur load-bearing — the player waits because recognition is the task, not because a timer is running.
  • It makes the party hint a real clue rather than a spoiler: a party narrows three names to one or two, it does not hand over the answer. Except in option 3 — see there.

The shape, in all three

  • One portrait, blurred. The vote is stated above it. No name appears on or under the portrait — the names live only in the answer options. That is the fix for the flaw and it is not negotiable in any of the three.
  • Three name options below. Blur decreases over time. A party-hint control is available.

Carried forward from the last pass — still binding

  • Blur steps 13 → 7 → 3 → 1px, never reaching zero. The residual keeps it deliberate rather than looking like a load that finished.
  • ~1.4s per step, ~5.6s total, which is why this replaces a cascade rather than sitting inside one. One inverted round per issue.
  • The reward floors at the ordinary card's payout (+25), so waiting can never pay less than a normal guess.
  • The round may only deal MKs that have an illustration. Binding on every cascade still to be written.
  • Never blur to the point of distortion. Softening a real politician's face is fine; deforming one is not. 13px on a 168px portrait is soft, not warped.

Correcting the record: the portrait shortfall is closed

  • An earlier board said 11 of 14 MKs had no illustration. That figure is stale. It came from a v16-era count; v19's asset pass closed it. As of now manifest.json reports 21 of 21 data.js politicians with art and politicians_without_art: []. The constraint above is therefore satisfied by every MK currently in the data — it binds on the new MKs the 3 Sep voting sheet introduces, of whom 11 of 14 have no portrait. Same ratio, different set; recording it here so the old number does not resurface.

V21-1 · stepped blur, hint names the pictured MK's party

The blur drops in four visible steps and the step track says so. The hint reveals the party of the MK in the picture — one fact, which narrows three names to whichever of them belong to that party. Picking early pays more and the amount is shown before the choice, not after.

רמז למפלגה

Blur: 4 discrete steps, 13/7/3/1px, track under the portrait. Hint: the pictured MK's party — one fact. Names: full-width stack, name only. Early pick: paid, and the current value is on screen before the choice.

The steps are legible as steps, so a player understands they are trading a known amount for a clearer face rather than watching an opaque timer. The hint narrows without resolving: "יש עתיד" leaves one of these three, but on a board with two Likud names it would leave two.

Risk: showing the live payout before the choice is the one thing the game does nowhere else — §0 of the previous brief forbids advertising an award before a decision, precisely because it moves attention from the content to the points. This option breaks that rule deliberately, because here the amount is the decision. If that exception is not wanted, this becomes V21-2's treatment and the two options collapse.

V21-2 · continuous blur, hint tags all three options

The blur eases continuously rather than stepping, and the bar is a plain progress track with no numbers on it. The hint puts a party on each of the three name buttons. This is a very different hint and the difference matters — see the risk.

הח”כ הזה הצביע נגד — מי זה?

רמז למפלגה

Blur: continuous ease, plain bar, no step marks and no number. Hint: tags every option with its party. Names: full-width stack. Early pick: paid, but never advertised — the coin simply flies at the end.

The only option that keeps §0's rule intact: nothing about the award is on screen before the choice. It is also the quietest — a face slowly resolving with no counter on it is closest to the sticker language and furthest from a quiz show.

Risk, and it is the reason to draw this hint separately: tagging all three options can hand over the answer. If the three names are from three different parties and the player recognises the face's party from the picture — a Haredi MK, a keffiyeh, a knitted kippah — the tags map straight onto it and the round is over. V21-1's hint cannot do that, because it names a party without saying which button holds it. It also risks teaching a stereotype, which is a content problem, not a UI one.

V21-3 · stepped blur, no hint button — the party is the fourth step

The hint stops being a control the player spends and becomes the last thing the wait gives them. Steps 1–3 are blur alone; if the player is still there at step 4 the party appears beside the portrait on its own. Nothing is bought, nothing is advertised, and waiting has exactly one currency — time.

Blur: 4 steps. Hint: none to press — the party is step 4. Names: two-up grid, third full width, so the block is shorter and the portrait can be larger. Early pick: paid, stated as a sentence rather than a live number.

The simplest of the three and the only one with no second control at all: one decision, three buttons, a clock. It also removes the "did I waste my hint" question entirely, and with it the risk that a player hoards the hint and never uses it.

At the last step the residual 1px blur and the party arrive together. The portrait is still not fully sharp — that is the standing rule, and here it also stops the final step feeling like the answer being handed over.

Risk: a player who is going to guess immediately never sees the party at all, so the clue only reaches the people who least need it — the opposite of what a hint is for. It also gives the round no interactive beat between arriving and answering, which over eleven issues may read as passive. And it is the only one of the three that cannot be tuned per player: the other two let a confident player skip the hint and an unsure one take it.

This state must not ship, and drawing it is how we keep it that way. With no portrait there is nothing to un-blur, so the round's entire mechanic is absent and the player is asked to recognise an initials badge. The correct behaviour is not to deal the round at all for an MK without art — the standing constraint above. It is drawn because it becomes reachable the moment cascades exist for the new issues: 11 of the 14 MKs the 3 Sep voting sheet introduces have no portrait, so the first time those cascades are written this state is one missing guard away.

Where this leaves things

  • The three differ on who pays for the clue. V21-1 sells information for payout and shows the price; V21-2 gives the same clue for free but a stronger one, and never mentions the award; V21-3 has no clue to take — time gives it or nothing does.
  • The hint difference is the sharpest decision on the board. V21-1's hint names the party of the face; V21-2's tags the options. Only the second can end the round in one tap, and only the second risks teaching a face-to-party stereotype.
  • V21-1 breaks a standing rule — it shows an award value before a choice, which §0 forbids everywhere else in the game. It is the only one that does, and it does it because the amount is the decision rather than a garnish. If the exception is refused, V21-1 becomes V21-2.
  • All three are drawn with mk_lapid_609.webp at real blur values, so what is on screen is what the filter actually does at 13/7/3/1px on a 168px portrait — none of them deform the face.
